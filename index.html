SAME PLACE · DIFFERENT QUESTION

Binary versus index.

A binary model screens locations using rules. An index model orders locations by relative suitability. Use the UVic location you selected to see both answers together.

BINARY MODEL

Pass or fail

—

Every active threshold must pass. Multiplying 0/1 layers gives 0 if even one factor fails.

INDEX MODEL

Relative score

—

Weighted, standardized layers give a continuous score. A high score can still fail a hard threshold.

Select a place in the UVic food-truck tab to compare the two model outputs.

SOURCE NOTES

What the maps can—and cannot—say.

The Hugag grid is a teaching example. The UVic map uses real geographic layers with clearly labeled proxies and uncertainties.

LECTURE BASIS

Binary and index models

GEOG 328 Lecture 16 presents binary rasters combined by multiplication, index layers standardized and combined by weighted sum, the Hugag habitat example, and the UVic food-truck prompt. This studio follows those concepts and uses its own teaching grid.

ILLUSTRATIVE

Hugag exercise rasters

The 6 × 6 elevation surface is synthetic. Slope and aspect are derived from its elevation differences. It teaches overlay logic; it is not a real habitat map.

REAL TERRAIN

UVic elevation and slope

Elevation samples are from OpenTopoData’s Mapzen v1.1 DEM on a 25 × 27 grid. Slope is estimated from neighboring cells. The roughly 47–52 m sample spacing and source resolution of about 30 m or coarser cannot verify the grade of a truck stall.

REAL RULES

Truck access and permission

UVic says commercial vehicles need appropriate permits and marked parking. Vehicles cannot use the central quad, paths, trails, grass or turf. Food-service approval, accessible queue space, utilities and waste servicing need separate checks.
